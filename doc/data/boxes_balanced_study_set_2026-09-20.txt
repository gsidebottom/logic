# A family-balanced study set, and what this engine can do on it (2026-09-20)
#
# WHY.  Every engine A/B in this repo -- chrono, shrinking, restarts, the
# reduce grace period, the gap decomposition -- was measured on the 22
# classic instances in the A/B corpus.  Those cover about 10 families, 14 of
# the 22 come from just TWO (ISCAS circuits and random 3-SAT), none are
# drawn from a competition set, and the median solve time is 0.91 s against
# the 2026 main track's 85.5 s.  Neutral on that sample is not neutral.
#
# THE SET.  tools/gbd/study_set.py selects from the competition index
# already on disk, ranking within each family by RECORDED SOLVE TIME from a
# previous portfolio run and taking the slowest in a band -- the opposite of
# curate_balanced.py's smallest-first, which is right for a fitness loop and
# wrong for measuring search quality.  Per-family hard cap, alternating
# SAT/UNSAT.
#
#   97 instances across 69 of the competition's 117 families
#   53 SAT / 44 UNSAT, portfolio time median 68 s (range 2-295 s)
#   41 families contribute a single instance -- inherent, since the
#   competition's own median family size is 2 with 55 singletons of 117

=== What this means ===
#
# The engine can be measured on under a quarter of the competition's
# families, and one fifth of a balanced sample, at a budget we can afford.
# Everything concluded from the 22-instance corpus describes behaviour on
# the easiest tail of the easiest families.
#
# The sharpest illustration is `belpyramid-puzzle`.  It is the family the
# old corpus had EIGHT instances of -- c3540_k8 and c5315_k8 with three
# shuffles each -- and it appears above in the list of families we solve
# nothing in.  The label lumps ISCAS-85 c3540/c5315 (2-4 K variables) in
# with the pyramid puzzles proper (75-380 K variables), so the old corpus
# was sampling the two smallest, least typical members of a family whose
# real instances are out of reach.  A "family" of eight was one circuit,
# shuffled.
#
# Note also what is NOT solvable here: circuit-multiplier,
# karatsuba-multiplication, matrix-multiplication, equivalence-checking,
# miter, prime-factoring, tseitin-formulas, cardinality-constraints,
# relativized-pigeon-hole.  Those are the families the box thesis is aimed
# at, and at 120 s the engine cannot reach any of them.
#
# Where it does well is worth recording too: it beats the portfolio's
# recorded time on ramseycube (0.7 s vs 66 s), graph-isomorphism (3.9 vs
# 164), argumentation (7.0 vs 241) and floodit-puzzle (30 vs 80), and
# trails badly on agile, subgraph-isomorphism and planning.  Those are
# different families from the ones the old corpus contained, which is the
# point of building this.
