# Certified preprocessing: SAT sweeping and select factoring with DRAT proofs (2026-09-29)
#
# doc/data/aig_rewriting_2026-09-27.txt ended with three multiplier-
# verification instances of the 2026 main track solved by ABC's dc2 + fraig
# in front of CaDiCaL (every arm of our 5000 s records times out on them,
# the 2026 winner's pipeline included; the competition's selection lists
# their status as unknown), and with two gaps: ABC's step is not
# certifiable, and ABC fails
# on the larger instances.  This file is the attempt to close both.
#
# === 1. Certified SAT sweeping: cnf2aig --sweep ===
#
# Functional equivalences between the gates of the input, each proved by
# CaDiCaL, the derivation written as a DRAT prefix: prefix + the solver's
# proof of the output refutes the input.  No ABC.
#
#   candidates   64-bit parallel random simulation of the gates (16 words);
#                classes of nodes no pattern tells apart, up to complement
#                (partition refinement); a counterexample is simulated at
#                once with 63 patterns near it
#   order        level by level; the first of a class is the representative
#   the question the definitions below the pair, merged gates replaced by
#                their representatives, in CaDiCaL (plain configuration,
#                proof recorded in memory through a Tracer), two calls
#                under assumptions, one per direction: first a window of 64
#                gates in a throwaway solver, then the whole cones in a
#                solver that is kept while it holds at most twice the cone
#   the proof    the solver's derived clauses, then the clause of the
#                negated assumptions; the solver's lemmas are deleted when
#                it goes
#   the ending   substituted clauses of the gates still read, as lemmas;
#                everything replaced is deleted
#
# The proof argument and its hole.  The checker holds the definitions in
# their original variables and follows the solver's unit propagation
# through the equivalences already derived.  That fails where two literals
# of a clause have become one: the solver's clause is unit while the
# checker's still has two literals.  The self-test found it (17 of the
# first 48 proofs rejected); those shortened clauses are now lemmas
# themselves, written when the gate is reached.
#
# Gate (tools/selftest_cnf2aig.py): generated instances -- a random circuit
# twice in different decompositions and a miter, the same with a planted
# difference, and-heavy circuits of up to 250 nodes, parity constraints that
# close definition cycles, nested multiplexers in three encodings -- under
# 15 settings, from a window of one gate to unlimited.  Every UNSAT answer
# with a proof that drat-trim accepts against the ORIGINAL, every SAT
# answer with a model that extends to the original, every verdict the
# original's.  0 failures in 15,000 checks (11,093 proofs, 3,907 models).
#
# === 2. What the sweep does on gm16spwtrc (320,194 gates) ===
#
#                                              seconds  merged constant refuted  no answer   proof
#   windows of at most 1,024 gates                249   1,843     190     448     89,287    17 MB
#   whole cones, a solver per attempt, 1,000      907*  6,345     327     189      3,628    31 MB
#   the same, 50,000 conflicts                  2,408*  6,568     469     191        956   282 MB
#   a solver that is kept, level order, 1,000     906*  6,433     393     189      4,274   238 MB
#   the same, 100 conflicts                       606*  5,614     198     170      5,320    48 MB
#   (* cut by --seconds; the last 10,000 gates, with cones of 100,000 gates
#   and more, were not finished)
#
# - Windows alone decide little: a model of part of a cone refutes nothing,
#   so a false candidate is tried again at every gate of its class.  95% of
#   the attempts ended that way.  With whole cones 189 counterexamples
#   settle what 87,401 window attempts could not.
# - 2% of the gates merge.  Fifty times the conflict budget buys 4% more
#   merges.  What is left is out of reach: two classes, the constant and
#   one output, account for 1,238 of 3,740 failed attempts (--give-up).
# - A call costs what the solver holds, not what the question is about:
#   45 ms per call with 117,758 gates held for cones of 10,000.  Hence the
#   bound on what the kept solver may hold.
#
# === 3. The ablation: it is the rewriting ===
#
# gm16spwtrc, ABC script in front of CaDiCaL 3.0.1, 5000 s:
#   dc2               ABC 102 s    409,059 ANDs   UNSAT in 1,882 s   1,869,943 conflicts
#   dc2; fraig; dc2   ABC 439 s    377,338 ANDs   UNSAT in 2,176 s
#   fraig             ABC not done after 3,600 s
#   none              (2026-09-28)                 timeout
# A first run of this ablation was void: both arms were the input, byte
# for byte (§5).
#
# What dc2 does.  gm16spwtrc is 320,194 multiplexers, each selected by a
# primary input, one unit and one clause of 32 literals over 18 outputs: a
# decision diagram that is not reduced.  Before dc2, 2 AND nodes of the
# graph read two primary inputs; after it 310 do, 264 distinct pairs, 254
# of them one input from each operand, with 170,481 readers between them,
# and the primary inputs are read 16,420 times instead of 761,705.  dc2
# recovers the partial products x_i * y_j, which the CNF spells as pairs of
# nested multiplexers:  s ? (s2 ? a : e) : e.
#
# === 4. Select factoring with a proof: cnf2aig --factor ===
#
#   o = s ? (s2 ? a : e) : e     ->     o = (s and s2) ? a : e

# === 6. Validating ABC by sweeping: no ===

# The other way to certify a rewriting: leave it to ABC, add the rewritten

# dc2's result (729,251 gates, 100 conflicts per call, 1,510 s): 67,588
# merges, 2,452 of the 3,526 output pairs proved equal -- and 2 of the 18
# outputs the clause is about.  The pairs near the outputs are the ones no
# budget reaches (§2).
#
# === 7. Defects found on the way ===
#
# - The gate detector invented inputs.  A gate found by truth table took as
#   inputs every variable of the candidate subset, mentioned by its clauses
#   or not; which one depended on the order of literals within the clauses.
#   With the literals sorted by value (the linear root simplifier of
#   bf960a3) the two base gates of gm16spwtrc got a gate downstream as an
#   input, and all 320,194 gates were on a cycle: the tool wrote its input
#   back, and an ablation ran CaDiCaL twice on the same file.  Caught by
#   reading the bridge log while the solvers ran.  tools/cnf2aig.py had the
#   same defect and the luckier order.
# - Every gate downstream of a definition cycle was dropped with it.  Broken
#   at one gate now.  Gates kept: gus-md5-14 5,463 -> 62,699 of 66,368,
#   gus-md5-15 5,387 -> 62,826 of 66,446.
#   For the experiment of 2026-09-28 this means that gus-md5 (two
#   instances), sncf_model_ixl_bmc_depth_15 and slp-synthesis-aes-bottom22
#   went to ABC with 7-80% of their gates: their timeouts say little.
# - Tautologies were tested on literals sorted by value, where a literal
#   and its negation are not neighbours.
# - An empty clause in the input was dropped, which made the output
#   satisfiable.  Found by chaining two modes.
# - ABC.  The old `fraig` dies with SIGBUS in Msat_ClauseCreate (its own
#   SAT solver's memory manager) after 31 minutes and 3.0 GB on gm24spctrc:
#   reproduced, with a crash report.  `&fraig` has a default of 1,000,000
#   conflicts per node and was not done after an hour.  Neither is needed
#   for these instances.

# === 10. The cut-based writer with a proof: cnf2aig --cuts (evening) ===
#
# What ABC's &write_cnf does, natively and with a derivation (commit
# 2a31456).  The gates that keep their variable are chosen for the fewest
# clauses: every gate gets its cuts from the cuts of what it reads (at most
# 6 leaves; functions as 64-bit truth tables), a cut costs the cubes of an
# irredundant cover of its function and of its complement (Minato-Morreale)
# plus a share of what its leaves cost (area flow, four rounds), and the
# choice starts at what the clauses outside the gates read.  A clause of a
# root follows from the definitions of the gates between the root and its
# cut: by unit propagation, else by cases on the cut's variables the
# clause does not mention.  A clause that is one of the root's own is kept.
# For that the function of a cut has to be the function of the gates
# between: put together from the cuts of what a gate reads, a cut can hold
# a gate and, on another path, what that gate reads, and the function put
# together the same way is right only where the two agree -- sound as a
# formula, not derivable from the gates between; the self-test found it.
#
# The factored gm16spwtrc (181,983 gates, 1,084,297 clauses):
#     leaves   variables kept   clauses    literals    seconds
#       3        181,971        727,025    2,174,445     2.6
#       5        139,284        630,622    2,033,696     3.2
#       6        130,692        613,496    2,016,846     4.5
#     ABC's writer on the same circuit: 143,410, 638,908, 2,050,058.
# With 3 leaves a gate keeps its own inputs and the writer only drops
# clauses: a multiplexer's cover is four clauses, not six.
#
# CaDiCaL 3.0.1 at 5000 s, on a machine carrying 6 to 10 jobs on 10 cores
# (the runs of §8 carried at most 7):
#   gm16spwtrc, --factor then --cuts, 3 leaves     UNSAT in 1,583 s   2,463,113 conflicts
#   gm16spwtrc, --factor then --cuts, 6 leaves     UNSAT in 1,423 s   2,305,949
#   gm16spwtcl, the same, 6 leaves                 UNSAT in 4,400 s   3,263,594
#   gm20spctrc, the same, 6 leaves                 UNSAT in 1,969 s   3,149,606
