\section{Summary}\label{sec:summary}

We report \textbf{53 new schemes for multiplying two $3\times 3$
matrices with 23 multiplications, with coefficients in $\{-1,0,+1\}$}
--- valid over any commutative ring, the same object class as
Laderman's 1976 scheme. Each scheme is:

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=1pt]
\item \textbf{verified mod 2} against all 729 Brent equations,
\item \textbf{pairwise inequivalent} under the full de Groote symmetry
  group ($\mathrm{GL}(3,2)^3 \rtimes S_3$, exact witness-or-refutation
  checks),
\item \textbf{inequivalent to every published scheme}: all 17{,}376
  schemes of the Kauers--Heule--Seidl (HKS) database \emph{and} the four
  classics (Laderman 1976, Smirnov 2013, Oh--Kim--Moon 2013,
  Courtois--Bard--Hulme 2011),
\item \textbf{lifted to integer coefficients} in $\{-1,0,+1\}$ and
  verified \textbf{exactly over $\mathbb{Z}$} against all 729 integer
  Brent equations.
\end{enumerate}

Discovery cost: the 53 came out of one \textbf{6-minute single-threaded
run} of a neighborhood-walk pipeline (138 raw finds $\rightarrow$ 53
survived full-database novelty checking), plus ${\sim}40$ minutes of
certification compute. (All timings in this report are from one
machine: a Mac~mini with an Apple M4~Pro --- 10 performance + 4
efficiency cores --- and 64\,GB RAM, macOS.) For scale: the HKS
campaign that produced the 17{,}376-scheme database used ${\sim}35$
CPU-years (methods differ; see \S\ref{sec:caveats} caveats).

The engine behind the discovery is a \textbf{native-ANF stochastic
local search (SLS)}: the Brent system is kept as 729 cubic-XOR
constraints over the 621 real variables instead of a
${\sim}26{,}500$-variable CNF, which extends the seeded-repair horizon
by $\ge 5{,}000\times$ over yalsat-on-CNF and, with an exact GF(2)
``tensor closure'' move, solves 8/10 of the official HKS challenge-1
instances (yalsat's published record: 5/10).

\section{Background}\label{sec:background}

A bilinear scheme for $3\times 3$ matrix multiplication with $r$
products is a triple of coefficient tensors $\alpha, \beta, \gamma$:
\begin{align*}
M_m &= \Bigl(\textstyle\sum_{a,b} \alpha^{(m)}_{ab}\,A_{ab}\Bigr)\cdot
       \Bigl(\textstyle\sum_{c,d} \beta^{(m)}_{cd}\,B_{cd}\Bigr),
       \qquad m = 1,\dots,r\\
C_{pq} &= \textstyle\sum_{m} \gamma^{(m)}_{pq}\,M_m
\end{align*}
Correctness is equivalent to the \textbf{Brent equations}; over GF(2):
\begin{equation*}
\bigoplus_{m=1}^{r} \alpha^{(m)}_{ab} \wedge \beta^{(m)}_{cd} \wedge
\gamma^{(m)}_{pq} \;=\; \delta_{bc}\,\delta_{ap}\,\delta_{dq}
\qquad \forall (a,b,c,d,p,q)
\end{equation*}
i.e.\ 729 cubic XOR equations over $27r$ variables ($r{=}23$: 621
vars; 27 equations have RHS 1 --- the ``type-3'' / delta equations).
Any integer scheme reduces mod 2 to a GF(2) scheme; conversely a GF(2)
scheme \emph{may} lift to signs $\pm 1$ (HKS observe lifting rarely
fails).

Known landscape: $r{=}23$ achievable (Laderman 1976); best lower bound
19 (Bl\"aser 2003); \textbf{$r{=}22$ open in both directions}. Before
HKS (SAT 2019 / J.~Symbolic Computation 2021), only 4 inequivalent
$\{-1,0,1\}$ schemes were known; their local-search campaign found
${>}17{,}000$ more, all published in the Linz database this report
checks against.

\section{The pipeline}\label{sec:pipeline}

Five stages; each has a tool in \texttt{matmul/} and an exact command
in \S\ref{sec:verify}.

\subsection{Native-ANF SLS engine (\texttt{src/anf.rs}, binary
\texttt{anf})}\label{sec:engine}

The Brent system is represented natively as cubic-XOR (``ANF'')
constraints over the 621 real variables --- no Tseitin auxiliaries.
Flipping a variable touches exactly its 81 incident equations; a
monomial toggles iff its two partner bits are 1, so incremental
evaluation is $O(81)$ per flip (0.4--5M flips/s/core measured,
${\sim}10$M flips/s aggregate over 10 threads). Two policy regimes
matter (both WalkSAT-family):

\begin{itemize}
\item close repair (many bits frozen at a known scheme): WalkSAT/SKC,
  noise 0.2, init density 0.25;
\item pairing / from-scratch: probSAT, cb 2.5, \textbf{init density
  0.10} ($\approx$ the free-support density of real completions).
\end{itemize}

The engine's structural move is the \textbf{tensor closure}: the Brent
system is tri-linear, and every equation contains exactly one
variable-group of each tensor, so fixing two tensors decomposes the
third into 9 independent $81\times r$ GF(2) linear systems. A
consistent single-tensor closure therefore \emph{solves the instance
outright}, and each closure call is monotone. It runs as an injected
hook every $N$ flips (\texttt{--closure-every}).

Baselines on this machine (the Mac~mini defined in
\S\ref{sec:summary}): kissat cannot solve the $r{=}23$ CNF (unknown at
60\,s; it needs 41.6\,s to prove even 2$\times$2-in-6 UNSAT), matching
HKS's finding that CDCL fails here. yalsat (the HKS solver, v1.0.1
built from source) solves seeded-repair CNFs at their published
operating point (fix 414/621 bits: ${\sim}0.05$--0.2\,s) but
\textbf{times out at 300\,s at fix=300 and fix=250 --- instances the
native engine solves in 5\,ms and 60\,ms}. On the official challenge-1
instances (hardcoded pairing cores, no streamliners), native SLS +
closure solves \textbf{8/10} (best two: 0.019\,s and 0.069\,s; the
0.069\,s A-instance solution was additionally confirmed by planting
our 621 bits as units into \emph{their} CNF and running kissat:
SATISFIABLE). yalsat's published record on challenge 1 is 5/10 ``in a
few minutes''; same-machine yalsat probes: A and M time out at 300\,s,
4-4-4-4-1 solves in 0.62\,s (native: 0.019\,s).

\subsection{Discovery: neighborhood walk
(\texttt{matmul/walk.py})}\label{sec:walk}

HKS ``method 2'', compounding: maintain a pool (24 verified seed
schemes fetched from the Linz DB: 4 classics + 20 spanning 20
rank-pattern directories); repeatedly pick a pool scheme, freeze a
random 300 of its 621 bits, let the engine complete the remaining 321
from a random start; canon-dedupe completions (sort the 23 summands);
genuinely new schemes join the pool and become seeds themselves. Every
accepted scheme is re-verified against the Brent equations by code
independent of the search.

The committed run: \texttt{--minutes 6 --nfix 300 --runs 8 --rng 7},
single-threaded $\rightarrow$ \textbf{138 schemes, distinct after
summand sorting, at ${\sim}3$\,s/scheme}, accelerating as the pool
diversifies.

\subsection{Exact equivalence (\texttt{matmul/equiv.py})}\label{sec:equiv}

De Groote's symmetry group acts on schemes; ``new'' must mean ``new
modulo that group''. Key implementation fact: writing schemes as
summands $(A, B, \Ct)$ with $\Ct = \gamma^{\mathsf T}$, the group
action becomes the cyclic sandwich
\[
(P A Q^{-1},\; Q B R^{-1},\; R\,\Ct\,P^{-1})
\]
--- and then every constraint ``this summand maps to that one'' is
\textbf{linear} in the 27 unknown GF(2) bits of $(P,Q,R)$. Exact
equivalence testing = backtracking over summand matchings (pruned by
rank triples) + incremental GF(2) RREF + nullspace enumeration +
invertibility + full multiset check; ${\sim}$ms per pair. An invariant
fingerprint (multiset of per-summand sorted rank triples + pair-sum
rank triples) prunes almost all pairs first.

Self-tests: 12 random group elements applied to Laderman are (a) still
valid schemes and (b) recovered as equivalent-with-witness; Laderman
vs Smirnov is refuted. On the 138 finds: \textbf{129 distinct classes}
(9 pairs were product-reorderings/$G$-images of each other).

\subsection{Database novelty (\texttt{matmul/novelty.py},
\texttt{matmul/dbcheck.py} via agent)}\label{sec:novelty}

Two independent layers:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Rank-pattern absence.} The DB's 302 directory names
  encode per-summand rank types (legend constraint-solved from 20
  known dir$\leftrightarrow$scheme pairs; letters refine sorted rank
  triples by slot: a=(1,1,1), b/d/j=(1,1,2), c/g/s=(1,1,3),
  e/k/m=(1,2,2), f=(1,2,3), n=(2,2,2), w=(2,2,3); unknown letters
  treated as wildcards --- conservative). Rank patterns are
  $G$-invariants, so pattern-absence $\Rightarrow$ inequivalence.
  Control: Laderman's pattern is absent from all 302 found-scheme dirs
  --- correct (HKS report their finds never reached Laderman's type).
\item \textbf{Full-database exact check.} The complete DB (single
  \texttt{schemes.tgz}, 43\,MB = \textbf{17{,}376 .tab files},
  byte-validated against live fetches) was converted
  ($\gamma$-transposed), with \textbf{0/17{,}376 parse failures} ---
  every file passes \texttt{verify\_bits==0} --- and 13/13 seed-anchor
  controls were recovered byte-identically. Per find: fingerprint
  comparison against all schemes in its compatible dirs, exact
  \texttt{equivalent()} on collisions. \textbf{Hardening:} all
  17{,}376 DB schemes were fingerprinted and the surviving finds
  re-checked ($\times 6$ $S_3$ variants) against the \emph{whole}
  database: \textbf{zero fingerprint matches anywhere}, making the
  verdicts independent of the legend.
\end{enumerate}

Verdicts (\texttt{matmul/novelty\_verdicts.csv}): \textbf{85 of 138
finds are equivalent to DB schemes} (expected --- the walk is
DB-seeded; witnesses recorded), \textbf{53 are new vs the entire
database}, and (from \S\ref{sec:equiv}'s audit) inequivalent to the
four classics and to each other: 53 finds = 53 classes.

\subsection{Integer lifting (\texttt{matmul/lift.py})}\label{sec:lift}

Hypothesis (HKS's): signs $\pm 1$ exist on the same support. Encoded
as \textbf{sign-SAT} rather than Gr\"obner bases: one sign bit per
support coefficient; a covering term's sign is the XOR of its three
sign bits; the integer Brent equation with $k$ covering terms and RHS
$r$ becomes ``exactly $(k-r)/2$ of the $k$ term-bits are 1'';
per-product scaling freedom is broken by fixing the first $\alpha$-
and $\beta$-support sign of each product. ${\sim}2{,}000$-clause CNFs;
kissat solves each in milliseconds. Every lift is then verified
\textbf{exactly over $\mathbb{Z}$} (integer arithmetic, all 729
equations) by code independent of the encoding.

Controls: all 4 classics lift and verify. Result: \textbf{53/53
lifted, zero failures}, outputs in \path{matmul/lifted/*.txt}.

\section{The 53 schemes}\label{sec:schemes}

Files: mod-2 bit-vectors \path{matmul/found/walk-*.bits} (the 53
names are the \texttt{NEW} rows of
\path{matmul/novelty_verdicts.csv}); signed integer forms
\path{matmul/lifted/walk-*.txt}. Support = number of nonzero
coefficients; for a $3{\times}3{\times}23$ scheme the naive addition
count is exactly support $-$ 55. Ours: support 149--164 (median 154)
= \textbf{94--109 naive additions}; for reference Laderman = 153/98,
Smirnov = 139/84, DB minimum = 139 (support percentiles of the full
DB: p1 = 146, median = 159 --- our sparsest sits at the 3rd
percentile; 22 of the 53 need fewer naive additions than Laderman,
none beats the DB's sparsest). Rank-type multiset = per-summand
sorted (rank $\alpha$, rank $\beta$, rank $\gamma$) with
multiplicities --- the invariant that separates 51 of the 53 from the
whole DB at the coarsest level.

\section{Secondary results}\label{sec:secondary}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Challenge 1 (HKS): 8/10 official instances solved}
  (pairing cores, no streamliners; yalsat's record 5/10). The two
  holdouts (M, 2-2-2-4-A) floor at best 3/729 after
  600\,s$\times$10 threads.
\item \textbf{Path-space SLS (connection-method local search): built,
  verified correct, measured NEGATIVE} at equal budget vs
  assignment-space SLS (orders of magnitude, all regimes). Diagnosis:
  the connection objective collapses (a conflicted variable hides
  force1$\times$force0 repairs) and the Brent matrix's sharing is
  dense (81 equations/var) --- path rerouting is myopic exactly where
  an assignment flip re-evaluates everything at once.
  \texttt{matmul/pathsls.py}, \texttt{doc/matmul\_plan.md} R3c.
\item \textbf{$r{=}22$ (challenge 4): open, probed.} Plain native
  attacks floor at 8/729. Drop-a-product repair floors at 1/729 ---
  but the finisher (\texttt{matmul/finisher22.py}) proved every such
  floor-1 state violates exactly the type-3 equation whose sole cover
  was dropped, is rigid to radius 3 (${\sim}1.3$M flip-sets), and
  fails per-tensor closure by exactly 1 row each: the floor-1 shell
  is a \emph{seeding artifact}, not evidence about $r{=}22$. Campaign
  infrastructure: \texttt{matmul/campaign22.py} (checkpointed; wave 1
  = 210 attacks logged).
\end{itemize}

\section{Caveats and scope}\label{sec:caveats}

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Re-checked 2026-07-04}: the 53 were additionally tested
  against Perminov's scheme collection
  (\url{https://github.com/dronperminov/FastMatrixMultiplication},
  the source of the Dec-2025 additive-record schemes; 7 distinct
  rank-23 schemes, all themselves HKS-DB classes) --- \textbf{zero
  matches; all 53 remain new}, with positive controls firing.
\item ``New'' = inequivalent under de Groote symmetry to the 17{,}376
  schemes in the Linz database snapshot (\texttt{schemes.tgz},
  Last-Modified 2020-08-07) and the 4 classics. The database is the
  comprehensive public record of $\{-1,0,1\}$ $3{\times}3{\times}23$
  schemes; we make no claim about unpublished or post-2020 private
  collections.
\item Discovery-effort comparisons with HKS (35 CPU-years vs minutes)
  are indicative, not controlled: we \emph{start from} their
  published schemes as seeds, they started from 4; their campaign
  also had to invent the methods. The like-for-like comparison is our
  seeded-repair and challenge-1 numbers vs yalsat on the same machine
  (\S\ref{sec:engine}).
\item The de-Groote checker, DB converter, and lift verifier are our
  code; their self-tests and controls are listed in
  \S\ref{sec:verify}. The two strongest external anchors: our
  A-instance solution satisfies \emph{HKS's own CNF} under kissat,
  and the DB fingerprint hardening uses only rank arithmetic + our
  exact checker with published inputs.
\item 51/53 schemes have four rank-(2,2,2) summands (like most HKS
  finds); none matches Laderman's four-quadruple core type.
\end{itemize}
