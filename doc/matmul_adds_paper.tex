\section*{Abstract}

\textbf{We multiply two $3\times 3$ matrices with 23 multiplications
and 55 additions} --- one below the previous record of 56 (Yinqi Sun,
arXiv:2604.27645, Apr 2026), and the first sub-56 scheme for exact
non-commutative $3\times3$ multiplication without change of basis. The
algorithm is short and concrete: form 13 signed combinations of the 9
entries of $A$ and 14 of $B$, take 23 products (each one
$A$-combination times one $B$-combination), and recombine them with 28
further additions into the 9 entries of $C$ --- negation is free. It is
a runnable program (\path{src/mm55.rs}; also
\path{matmul/external/i19-55adds-slp.txt}) checked against the naive
27-multiplication triple loop on 500{,}000 random integer inputs
\textbf{and} 50{,}000 non-commutative $2\times2$-block inputs --- so it
holds over any ring and recurses on block matrices --- with an
operation counter confirming exactly $23\times$ and $55\pm$
(\texttt{cargo test mm55}). Its correctness is additionally
machine-checked in Lean~4 (\texttt{matmul/mm55proof/}): a sorry-free
proof that, over a general non-commutative ring, the program's 9
outputs are the entries of $A\cdot B$ --- and, in Mathlib's own
\texttt{Matrix} API, that the scheme equals \texttt{A * B}.

The 56 record was the end of a rapid chain --- 60 (Stapleton) $\to$ 59
(M\aa rtensson--Stankovski Wagner--Stapleton, MWS) $\to$ 58 $\times 3$
(Perminov) $\to$ 56 --- every step driven by greedy
common-subexpression elimination (CSE) on the linear forms. A scheme's
additive cost splits into two \emph{input sides} (the left/right
linear factors of the 23 products) and one \emph{output side} (the 9
outputs recombined from the products); greedy pair-extraction CSE
returns only an \emph{upper bound} per side. We minimize both sides
\textbf{exactly}:

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Input sides} (\texttt{matmul/sidemin.py}): the minimum
  $\pm$-additions such that one addition chain over the 9 input entries
  covers every distinct factor row up to sign --- Sun's chain-covering
  structure --- by iterative deepening over helper values. It
  reproduces Sun's own 13+13 optima and both of his per-side
  impossibility certificates.
\item \textbf{Output side} --- the new ingredient
  (\texttt{matmul/tcmin.py}): the \textbf{transposition principle}
  (Tellegen). For any addition-only linear map, $A(W) = A(\WT) +
  (\mathrm{inputs} - \mathrm{outputs})$; the $9\times23$ output map $W$
  therefore costs $A(\WT) + 14$, and \WT{} is a 9-input ternary map ---
  \emph{exactly the regime the input-side minimizer solves exactly},
  and small ($A(\WT) \approx 16$). So the output side is minimized
  \textbf{exactly and constructively} (transpose the chain), where
  greedy CSE --- the engine of the entire $60\to56$ chain --- fell
  short by an addition.
\end{enumerate}

Combining the two on Perminov's \texttt{cn122} scheme (class
\texttt{i19w225c4efh}, published at 58 additions, a de Groote class
distinct from Sun's) gives exact output side 28 (greedy: 29) with input
sides 13+14, i.e.
\[
  \mathbf{55 = 13 + 14 + 28,}
\]
the first sub-56 scheme. It is verified exactly (Brent equations over
$\mathbb{Z}$) and functionally by an independent from-scratch checker
--- 5{,}000 random integer $3\times3$ products \textbf{and} 300
non-commutative $2\times2$-block products, operation count exactly 55.
The same exact method independently confirms \textbf{Sun's own
representative is output-optimal} (56 exact): 56 was optimal for his
class, and 55 lives on a different class that had never been exactly
output-minimized.

\section{Notation and terminology}\label{sec:notation}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Sides.} A scheme's additive cost splits into two
  \emph{input sides} --- the $A$- and $B$-side forms, over the 9
  entries of $A$ and of $B$ --- plus one \emph{output side}, the
  $C$-side forms computing the 9 outputs from the 23 products. Both are
  minimized exactly here: input sides by chain covering
  (\S\ref{sec:in}), the output side by transposition
  (\S\ref{sec:out}).
\item \textbf{de Groote group, class, orbit, representative.} The de
  Groote symmetry group acts on schemes (sandwiching the three tensors
  by invertible matrices, plus the $S_3$ slot symmetries); two schemes
  are \emph{equivalent} if one is the image of the other. The
  \textbf{orbit} of a scheme is its set of images under the group ---
  identical to its equivalence \textbf{class}; a \textbf{representative}
  is any member. The additive cost varies across a class, but never the
  class itself.
\item \textbf{Scheme identifiers} (\texttt{i12w219c23ci-008},
  \texttt{i2w201c26fi-000}, \dots) are the file names of the stored
  schemes in the public HKS database, used here verbatim as opaque
  labels; the trailing \texttt{-N} indexes files sharing a prefix, and
  the prefix fields encode HKS's own cataloguing invariants (see the
  HKS database reference). Nothing relies on their semantics: ``class
  $X$'' always means the de Groote equivalence class of the scheme
  stored under that name, and every class identity we assert is
  established by our own exact fingerprint + witness machinery, not by
  name.
\item \textbf{sign-SAT} (companion paper, \S 3.5): the Boolean
  satisfiability instance whose variables are the signs of the
  mod-2-supported coefficients. A covering term's sign is the XOR of
  its three factor signs, and each integer Brent equation with $k$
  covering terms and right-hand side $\delta$ becomes the cardinality
  constraint ``exactly $(k-\delta)/2$ of the $k$ terms are negative''.
  Its solutions are exactly the valid $\pm 1$ lifts of the mod-2 scheme
  (worked example in Appendix~C).
\item \textbf{$\mathbb{Z}$-verified}: checked exactly over the integers
  --- all 729 Brent equations evaluated with the scheme's $\pm 1$
  coefficients in exact integer arithmetic, zero residuals. (Code
  output writes plain \texttt{Z} for $\mathbb{Z}$.)
\item \textbf{$h^{*}$}: the star denotes the optimal value --- $h^{*}$
  is the smallest helper count $h$ at which the side-minimizer's search
  succeeds.
\item \textbf{Closure normal form}: defined in \S\ref{sec:in}
  (Method); worked example in Appendix~B.
\end{itemize}

\section{Cost model and history}\label{sec:model}

A bilinear scheme with $r$ products computes $M_m = P_m \cdot Q_m$ from
linear forms $P_m$ (over the 9 entries of $A$), $Q_m$ (over $B$), then
each $C$ entry as a linear form over the $M_m$. The \textbf{additive
complexity} is the number of binary additions/subtractions in a
straight-line program (SLP) computing all forms; unary negation is
free; no change of basis (Karstadt--Schwartz accounting is \emph{not}
used). This is the model of M\aa rtensson--Wagner, Stapleton, Perminov,
and Sun; Schwartz--Vaknin's 61 lives in the weaker with-basis-change
model.

\section{The exact input-side minimizer
(\texttt{matmul/sidemin.py})}\label{sec:in}

\textbf{Problem.} Given the 23 signed factor rows of one side (vectors
in $\{-1,0,+1\}^9$; rows equal to a basis vector or to $\pm$another row
are free), find the minimum number of steps of the form $v = \pm x \pm
y$ ($x, y$ earlier values, starting from the 9 basis vectors) such that
every distinct multi-term row appears among the values up to global
sign. This is Sun's chain-covering structure as a search problem: a
single addition chain whose values include all 23 left (or right)
factors, where greedy pair extraction can only build balanced trees of
shared \emph{pairs} and cannot reuse a sum of three, four, or five
inputs.

\textbf{Method.} Iterative deepening on the number of \emph{helpers}
$h$ (chain values that are not target rows); the optimum is
(\#distinct multi-term targets) $+\, h^{*}$. The search uses what we
call the \textbf{closure normal form}: because the pool of computed
values only ever grows, covering a currently-derivable target can never
hurt a later step and always costs exactly one addition --- so the
search covers every derivable target greedily until none remains (the
\emph{closure fixpoint}) and branches \textbf{only on which helper
value to insert at a fixpoint}. Any optimal chain reorders into this
form (an exchange argument: coverable-target steps commute forward past
helper insertions), so restricting the search to it loses nothing. With
one helper left, the helper must directly complete some uncovered
target, shrinking the last level to an ``enabling set''. Helpers may be
arbitrary integer vectors, doublings included --- strictly more general
than the pure/one-aux model of Sun's certificates, so an exhaustion at
slack $h$ here is the stronger impossibility statement.

\textbf{Calibration against Sun's own certificates.} His verifier
proves each of his sides optimal by a reachability argument (no
12-addition chain covers $U$'s 12 targets; no 12-addition chain with
any single auxiliary covers $V$'s 11). Our search independently
reproduces all of it --- $U$: 12 targets, $h^{*}=1$, 13 additions (3
search nodes); $V$: 11 targets, $h^{*}=2$, 13 additions (11 nodes); the
$h=0$ and $h=1$ exhaustions match his two impossibility certificates
--- in 0.3\,s total, every chain replay-verified. Micro-optima (5
hand-checkable cases) also pass.

\section{The exact output-side minimizer (transposition
principle)}\label{sec:out}

The output side computes the 9 result entries $C_{pq} = \sum_m
W_{pq,m} M_m$ from the 23 products: a linear map $W$, a $9\times23$
ternary matrix. Greedy pair-extraction CSE minimizes it only
approximately (an upper bound). The exact minimum is the \emph{shortest
linear straight-line program} for $W$ --- NP-hard in general, and the
reason the record chain never drove the output side to its true
minimum.

\textbf{The transposition principle} (Tellegen; standard in the
linear-circuit and signal-processing literature, and in algebraic
complexity theory) resolves it. For an addition-only linear map ---
only $\pm$ of earlier values, no change of basis, negation free --- the
minimum additions of a map and its transpose differ by a fixed
constant:
\[
  A(W) = A(\WT) + \bigl(\mathrm{inputs}(W) - \mathrm{outputs}(W)\bigr).
\]
(We verify the constant on hand-checkable cases: for $M$ with
$y_1=x_1{+}x_2$, $y_2=x_2{+}x_3$, $A(M)=2$ and $A(M^{\mathsf T})=1 =
2+(2-3)$; \texttt{tcmin.py}'s selftest checks four such.) For the output
side $W$ is $9\times23$ (23 inputs, 9 outputs), so $\mathbf{A(W) =
A(\WT) + 14}$, and \WT{} is a 23-output map over \textbf{9 inputs} ---
\emph{exactly the shape the input-side minimizer solves exactly}, and
small ($A(\WT) \approx A(W) - 14 \approx 16$). Hence
\[
  \text{exact output side} = \texttt{sidemin}(\text{rows of }\WT
  \text{ over 9 dims}) + 14,
\]
computed in milliseconds. It is \textbf{constructive}: transposing the
\WT{} addition chain by the standard adjoint (reverse the program;
fan-out $\leftrightarrow$ addition) yields an explicit
$A(\WT){+}14$-addition program for the 9 outputs, which
\texttt{matmul/tcmin.py} emits and which we verify independently
(Appendix~A).

\textbf{Impact.} On Sun's own representative the exact output side is
30 --- equal to his greedy count, so \textbf{Sun's scheme is
output-optimal and 56 is optimal for his representative}. But on
Perminov's \texttt{cn122} representative (class \texttt{i19w225c4efh})
the exact output side is \textbf{28}, where greedy CSE returns 29 ---
the missing addition greedy could not see. Combined with the exact
input sides 13+14, that is a \textbf{55-addition scheme}
(Appendix~A). \texttt{tcmin.py}'s selftest pins both numbers (Sun 30,
cn122 28) and the transposition constant.

\item \textbf{55 on one class} (\texttt{i19w225c4efh}) --- the record,

  on 864 representatives of this one class.
\item \textbf{56 on two classes} (\texttt{i46w213c23ci} =
  Sun's, and \texttt{i12w219c23ci}); before the exact re-score these
  read 56/58 under greedy. The exact output side beats greedy by one
  addition on the two Perminov classes (cn122 $29\to28$, cn119
  $30\to29$) and by one on the cn120 representative ($28\to27$).
\item \textbf{No 54 exists anywhere in the published database ---
  55 is the additive minimum.} Two exhaustive tiers, both
  model-independent via the GF(2) bound:
  \begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{Slim representatives} (GF(2) input sides $\le 27$,
    possible only in the 4 floor-26 + 80 floor-27 classes): all
    79{,}488 were enumerated; GF(2) sides + exact GF(2) output side
    $\ge 55$ on every one (and the 12-sign-model exact $\mathbb{Z}$
    re-score independently bottoms out at 55).
  \item \emph{Fat representatives} (input sides $\ge 28$) of
    \textbf{every} class: the output-side cost depends only on the
    $\gamma$-support, whose de Groote orbit reduces to 784 canonical
    $(X \otimes Y)$-images per tensor slot (output-coordinate
    permutations are free, so $X, Y$ range over $S_3$-cosets of
    $\mathrm{GL}(3,2)$; validated against the full 56{,}448-image
    sweep). \texttt{matmul/cfloor.py} swept all 44{,}125 candidate
    slots of all 17{,}376 classes: \textbf{no image anywhere has XOR
    cover $\le 12$}, so the exact GF(2) output side is $\ge 27$ on
    every representative of every class, and any fat representative
    totals $\ge 28 + 27 = 55$.
  \end{enumerate}
  Exactly 620 slots (616 classes, including the record class and
  Sun's) reach cover 13 --- output side 27 --- so those are the only
  places further (record-tying, never record-beating) 55s could
  live.

  \emph{Scope extension (2026-07-11).} Rational flip-walk exploration
  (the \texttt{flip23} engine; companion notes in this repository)
  later produced the first additions to the known rank-23 corpus
  beyond the database and our 53 lifted classes: \textbf{46 new de
  Groote classes}, harvested from overnight random walks and
  certified inequivalent by rank-type invariants plus exact matching.
  The same two-tier machinery closes them all with margin: orbit
  input-side floors are 30--32 (fatter than every hot database
  class), and the strict-target $\gamma$-orbit sweep (XOR cover
  $\le 55 - \text{floor} - 14$) admits no image on any slot ---
  \textbf{every representative of every new class totals $\ge 56$}.
  A further 35 classes from \emph{reuse-capped} walks (support-thin
  storms designed to steer toward low-addition territory) closed
  identically --- floors 30--32, the same distribution as
  unconstrained walks --- so representative-level thinness does not
  reach the low-floor island. The 55 minimum, and the record class's
  uniqueness at 55, stand over the enlarged catalogue of
  $17{,}376 + 53 + 46 + 35$ classes.
\end{itemize}

\section{Contributions}\label{sec:contrib}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{A 55-addition rank-23 scheme} --- the first below 56 ---
  with an explicit, runnable, independently re-verified program
  (\path{src/mm55.rs}, \path{matmul/external/i19-55adds-slp.txt}).
\item \textbf{Exact two-sided additive minimization} in this model: the
  input side as addition-chain covering (\texttt{sidemin.py},
  calibrated against Sun's own optimality certificates), and the output
  side by the \textbf{transposition principle} (\texttt{tcmin.py}) ---
  the ingredient the entire greedy-CSE record chain lacked. Both are
  exact and constructive.
\item \textbf{An exact re-score of the record chain}, which improves
  every class on its own representative and fixes the true count per
  class (\S\ref{sec:results}): 56 on two classes, 55 on one, and
  confirmation that Sun's representative was already output-optimal.
\item \textbf{An additive-optimality theorem for the published
  catalogue}: no representative of any of the 17{,}376 published
  classes, under any sign model, multiplies $3\times3$ matrices with
  23 products in fewer than 55 additions (\S\ref{sec:results}) --- so
  the record is not just current but final for this catalogue, and any
  future sub-55 scheme must come from a class outside it.
\end{itemize}

\section{Reproduction}\label{sec:repro}

Prerequisites: \texttt{python3} (3.11+, no packages needed),
\texttt{kissat} (sign-model enumeration; e.g.\ \texttt{brew install
kissat}), a Rust toolchain (\texttt{cargo}) for the runnable scheme,
and \texttt{elan} for the Lean proof. The database-wide steps
additionally need the public scheme archive --- rebuild it from the
primary source with \texttt{python3 dbcheck.py fetch \&\& python3
dbcheck.py convert} (downloads \texttt{schemes.tgz}, ${\sim}43$\,MB,
from the Linz algebra server and writes
\texttt{dbcache/all\_schemes.txt} behind a verify-everything gate).

\begin{Code}

# the record, end to end: exact sides + transposition-exact output,

python3 tcmin.py perminov_cache/bits/sun56.bits \

\section{Future work}\label{sec:future}

Both sides are now minimized exactly, so the total for any given
representative is its true additive complexity in this model. The open
question is \textbf{whether 54 exists}:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{More 55s (never fewer) inside the catalogue.} The 616
  classes whose $\gamma$-orbit reaches output side 27
  (\S\ref{sec:results}) are the only places a further, fat-sided
  $55 = 28 + 27$ could live; pairing each with exact input sides at
  its cover-13 images is a bounded, well-defined hunt for
  record-\emph{tying} schemes.
\item \textbf{Beyond the published catalogue.} The optimality theorem
  is relative to the 17{,}376 published classes; our own 53 novel
  classes clear it with margin (every representative has output side
  $\ge 28$ and input sides $\ge 28$, so $\ge 56$ total --- they
  cannot even host a 55), and the 46 further classes since discovered
  by rational flip walks clear it the same way (floors 30--32,
  \S\ref{sec:results} scope extension). A sub-55 scheme, if one exists, requires a
  rank-23 class nobody has catalogued --- the de Groote classification
  at rank 23 is not known to be complete --- or a cost model beyond
  $\pm1$ coefficients. For the latter we can already close the
  nearest door: in the adds+shifts model (unit-cost scalings by $2^k$,
  the model of the $4\times4$ literature), the exact minima of this
  scheme's three maps are \emph{unchanged} --- still $13 + 14 + 28 =
  55$, and still 56 for Sun's scheme (\texttt{matmul/shiftmin.py}, an
  exact extension of the chain-covering minimizer validated against
  exhaustive search). A sub-55 via richer coefficients therefore
  requires a genuinely dyadic \emph{scheme}, not a cheaper program
  for the known ones.
\item \textbf{Independent certification.} The GF(2) floors rest on our
  minimizers (selftested against exhaustive search, quotient validated
  against the full orbit); DRAT-certified UNSAT instances of the same
  statements (companion benchmark paper) would make them
  solver-checkable end to end.
\item \textbf{Database-wide exact re-score.} The input-side floors of
  all 17{,}376 classes are already charted (in the git-history census);
  pairing the low-floor classes with exact output sides is the
  systematic search for a second record.
\item \textbf{Output-side lower bounds.} The exact output minimum is
  now known per representative via transposition; certifying it as a
  \emph{class} optimum (that no representative does better) is a
  separate SAT/ILP problem --- the subject of the companion benchmark
  note \path{doc/matmul_cxlb_satcomp.md}.
\item The rank-22 question (challenge 4 of the HKS
  matrix-multiplication challenges,
  \url{https://github.com/marijnheule/matrix-challenges}) remains open
  and is where any result would change complexity theory rather than
  constants.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Appendix B: the side-minimizer on a worked example}

Rows (targets) over base variables $a, b, c, d$: $T_1 = a + b$ and
$T_2 = a + b + c + d$. Both have $\ge 2$ terms, are distinct up to
global sign, and neither is a basis vector, so $\mathrm{nt} = 2$ and any
chain needs at least 2 additions.

\textbf{Slack $h = 0$} (no helpers --- every addition must create a
target): the pool starts as $\{a, b, c, d\}$. $T_1 = a + b$ is
derivable (both operands in the pool): cover it; the pool grows to
$\{a, b, c, d, a{+}b\}$. Now $T_2$ must be $x \pm y$ with $x, y$ in the
pool: subtracting each pool value from $T_2$ gives $c{+}d$, $a{+}c{+}d$,
$b{+}c{+}d$, $a{+}b{+}c$, $a{+}b{+}d$ --- none in the pool, so the
closure is at a fixpoint with $T_2$ uncovered, and $h = 0$ is
\textbf{exhausted: 2 additions are impossible.}

\textbf{Slack $h = 1$}: at that same fixpoint the \emph{enabling set}
--- values $u$ that would directly complete an uncovered target as
$T_2 = x \pm u$ --- is $\{c{+}d,\ a{+}c{+}d,\ b{+}c{+}d,\ a{+}b{+}c,\
a{+}b{+}d, \dots\}$; of these, $u = c + d$ is itself derivable from the
pool ($c$ and $d$ are basis vectors). Insert it as the helper, and the
closure resumes: $T_2 = (a{+}b) + (c{+}d)$.

The optimum is $\mathrm{nt} + h^{*} = 2 + 1 = \textbf{3 additions}$,
found in 3 search nodes; the emitted chain is

\begin{Code}
w0 = a + b        (covers T1)
w1 = c + d        (helper)
w2 = w0 + w1      (covers T2)
\end{Code}

\noindent replay-verified by expansion. Global signs are free
throughout: a row $-a - b$ is the same target as $a + b$ (a chain value
may be used negated at no cost).

\section*{Appendix C: exact scoring end to end (sun56)}

The pipeline of \S\ref{sec:in}--\S\ref{sec:out} on Sun's
representative, every number reproduced by \texttt{sidemin.py --models
24 perminov\_cache/bits/sun56.bits} and \texttt{tcmin.py
perminov\_cache/bits/sun56.bits}:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Mod-2 gate.} The 621-bit vector satisfies all 729 Brent
  equations over GF(2).
\item \textbf{Sign-SAT.} The support has 175 nonzero coefficients (175
  sign variables); the 729 integer Brent equations have 453 covering
  terms in total, and the instance has 4{,}269 clauses. Two concrete
  equations: one type-3 equation is covered by $k = 3$ terms (products
  $M_2$, $M_{17}$, $M_{23}$) with right-hand side 1, so \textbf{exactly
  $(3-1)/2 = 1$ of the three terms must be negative}; a neighboring
  rhs-0 equation is covered by $k = 4$ terms ($M_2$, $M_{17}$,
  $M_{20}$, $M_{23}$), so exactly 2 of 4 are negative. kissat solves
  the instance in milliseconds; each returned model is
  $\mathbb{Z}$-verified before use.
\item \textbf{Exact input sides} (model m0). The 23 $A$-rows contain 12
  distinct multi-term targets; $h = 0$ is exhausted and $h^{*} = 1$
  succeeds: $A$-side $= 12 + 1 = \textbf{13}$ (3 search nodes). The
  $B$-rows contain 11 distinct targets; $h = 0$ and $h = 1$ are
  exhausted, $h^{*} = 2$ succeeds: $B$-side $= \textbf{13}$ (11 nodes).
  These reproduce Sun's own input-side optima and impossibility
  certificates.
\item \textbf{Exact output side} (transposition). The transpose \WT{}
  (23 rows over the 9 outputs) has additive cost 16 by \texttt{sidemin},
  so the output side is $16 + 14 = \textbf{30}$ --- equal to greedy
  here, confirming Sun's representative is output-optimal.
\item \textbf{Total.} $13 + 13 + 30 = \textbf{56}$, the true additive
  complexity of Sun's representative.
\end{enumerate}
