\section*{Abstract}

The additive-complexity record for exact non-commutative $3\times 3$
matrix multiplication with 23 multiplications, without change of
basis, stood at \textbf{56 additions} (Yinqi Sun, arXiv:2604.27645,
Apr 2026) --- the end of a rapid chain 60 (Stapleton) $\to$ 59
(M\aa rtensson--Stankovski Wagner--Stapleton, MWS) $\to$ 58 $\times 3$
(Perminov) $\to$ 56, every step driven by greedy common-subexpression
elimination (CSE) on the linear forms. \textbf{We reduce it to 55.}

A scheme's additive cost splits into two \emph{input sides} (the
left/right linear factors of the 23 products) and one \emph{output
side} (the 9 outputs recombined from the 23 products). Greedy
pair-extraction CSE returns only an \emph{upper bound} for each side.
We minimize both sides \textbf{exactly}:

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Input sides} (\texttt{matmul/sidemin.py}): the minimum
  $\pm$-additions such that one addition chain over the 9 input
  entries covers every distinct factor row up to sign --- Sun's
  chain-covering structure --- by iterative deepening over helper
  values. It reproduces Sun's own 13+13 optima and both of his
  per-side impossibility certificates.
\item \textbf{Output side} --- the new ingredient
  (\texttt{matmul/tcmin.py}): the \textbf{transposition principle}
  (Tellegen). For any addition-only linear map, $A(W) = A(W^{\mathsf
  T}) + (\mathrm{inputs} - \mathrm{outputs})$; the $9\times23$ output
  map $W$ therefore costs $A(W^{\mathsf T}) + 14$, and $W^{\mathsf T}$
  is a 9-input ternary map --- \emph{exactly the regime the
  input-side minimizer solves exactly}, and small ($A(W^{\mathsf T})
  \approx 16$). So the output side is minimized \textbf{exactly and
  constructively} (transpose the chain), where greedy CSE --- the
  engine of the entire $60\to56$ chain --- fell short by an addition.
\end{enumerate}

Combining the two on Perminov's \texttt{cn122} scheme (class
\texttt{i19w225c4efh}, published at 58 additions, a de Groote class
distinct from Sun's) gives exact output side 28 (greedy: 29) with
input sides 13+14, i.e.
\[
  \mathbf{55 = 13 + 14 + 28,}
\]
the first sub-56 scheme. It is verified exactly (Brent equations over
$\mathbb{Z}$) and functionally by an independent from-scratch checker
--- 5{,}000 random integer $3\times3$ products \textbf{and} 300
non-commutative $2\times2$-block products, operation count exactly 55
--- so it is a genuine bilinear algorithm, not a commutativity trick
(Sun's own verification standard). The explicit program is
\path{matmul/external/i19-55adds-slp.txt} (Appendix~A). The same exact
method independently confirms \textbf{Sun's own representative is
output-optimal} (56 exact): 56 was optimal for his class, and 55 lives
on a different class that had never been exactly output-minimized.

The rest of this note is retained from the 56-era version (v1--v3):
the exact classification of the whole record chain against the
Heule--Kauers--Seidl (HKS) database (all five prior record schemes are
HKS classes), three independent ties of 56 on distinct classes (kept
for the method; \S\ref{sec:results} gives the exact re-score), and the
exhaustive orbit-wide side-floor census over all 17{,}376 database
classes (\S\ref{sec:census}). Every count uses the exact two-sided
minimizer; the open question is now \textbf{54} (\S\ref{sec:limits}).

\section{Notation and terminology}\label{sec:notation}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Sides.} A scheme's additive cost splits into two \emph{input sides} --- the $A$- and $B$-side forms, over the 9 entries of $A$ and of $B$ --- plus one \emph{output side}, the $C$-side forms computing the 9 outputs from the 23 products. The exact input-side minimizer of \S\ref{sec:minimizer} (the \emph{side-minimizer} for short) treats the two input sides; the output side is minimized exactly too, via the transposition principle (\S\ref{sec:trans}). Throughout, a bare ``sides'', a ``side floor'', and ``fat-/slim-sides'' refer to the \textbf{input} sides; the output side is always named explicitly.
\item \textbf{Cell.} The de Groote sandwich fixes each side of a representative from a pair of the sandwich matrices --- the output/$C$ side from the $(R,P)$ pair, the $A$ side from $(P,Q)$, the $B$ side from $(Q,R)$ (see \S\ref{sec:floors}). Ranging that pair over the 168 elements of $\mathrm{GL}(3,2)$ in each coordinate gives a $168\times168$ grid, and each entry is a \textbf{cell} --- one concrete side (its 9 forms) with that side's cost. It is the unit of the side-cost tables of \S\ref{sec:floors} (``sub-ms per cell''); unqualified, a \emph{cell} means an output-side cell, the object the $C$-side lower-bound search of \S\ref{sec:limits} decides.
\item \textbf{Scheme identifiers} (\texttt{i12w219c23ci-008},
  \texttt{i2w201c26fi-000}, \dots) are the file names of the stored
  schemes in the public HKS database, used here verbatim as opaque
  labels; the trailing \texttt{-N} indexes files sharing a prefix,
  and the prefix fields encode HKS's own cataloguing invariants (see
  the HKS database reference at the end of this note). Nothing in
  this note relies on their semantics:
  ``class $X$'' always means the de Groote equivalence class of the
  scheme stored under that name, and every class identity we assert
  is established by our own exact fingerprint + witness machinery,
  not by name.
\item \textbf{de Groote group, class, orbit, representative.} The
  de Groote symmetry group acts on schemes (sandwiching the three
  tensors by invertible matrices, plus the $S_3$ slot symmetries);
  two schemes are \emph{equivalent} if one is the image of the
  other. The \textbf{orbit} of a scheme is its set of images under
  the group --- identical to its equivalence \textbf{class}; a
  \textbf{representative} is any member. An \textbf{orbit walk} is
  a local search over representatives inside one class (moving by
  small group elements), which can change the additive cost but
  never the class.
\item \textbf{sign-SAT} (companion paper, \S 3.5): the Boolean
  satisfiability instance whose variables are the signs of the
  mod-2-supported coefficients. A covering term's sign is the XOR of
  its three factor signs, and each integer Brent equation with $k$
  covering terms and right-hand side $\delta$ becomes the
  cardinality constraint ``exactly $(k-\delta)/2$ of the $k$ terms
  are negative''. Its solutions are exactly the valid $\pm 1$ lifts
  of the mod-2 scheme (worked example in Appendix~C).
\item \textbf{$\mathbb{Z}$-verified}: checked exactly over the
  integers --- all 729 Brent equations evaluated with the scheme's
  $\pm 1$ coefficients in exact integer arithmetic, zero residuals.
  (Code output and command comments write plain \texttt{Z} for
  $\mathbb{Z}$.)
\item \textbf{$h^{*}$}: the star denotes the optimal value ---
  $h^{*}$ is the smallest helper count $h$ at which the
  side-minimizer's search succeeds.
\item \textbf{Closure normal form}: defined in
  \S\ref{sec:minimizer} (Method); worked example in Appendix~B.
\end{itemize}

\section{Cost model and history}\label{sec:model}

A bilinear scheme with $r$ products computes $M_m = P_m \cdot Q_m$
from linear forms $P_m$ (over the 9 entries of $A$), $Q_m$ (over
$B$), then each $C$ entry as a linear form over the $M_m$. The
\textbf{additive complexity} is the number of binary
additions/subtractions in a straight-line program (SLP) computing
all forms; unary negation is free; no change of basis
(Karstadt--Schwartz accounting is \emph{not} used). This is the
model of M\aa rtensson--Wagner, Stapleton, Perminov, and Sun;
Schwartz--Vaknin's 61 lives in the weaker with-basis-change model.

\section{Why 58 was the pair-extraction wall}\label{sec:wall}

Sun's $56 = 13 + 13 + 30$ ($A$-side + $B$-side + outputs). Both
input sides are \emph{chain-covering}: a single 13-step addition
chain per side whose values include all 23 factor rows --- every
$P_m$ is a bare reference into the chain. Greedy pair extraction
builds balanced binary trees of shared pairs; it cannot discover a
program in which intermediate \emph{sums of three, four, five
inputs} are themselves the reused objects. Measured consequence: our
v1 optimizer (and its v2 with kernel and output-reuse moves --- both
measured, kernels net-harmful, reuse neutral here) plateaus at
\textbf{58 = 14+14+30 on Sun's own representative}, exactly $1{+}1$
above his sides, with an identical output side. The two missing
additions were purely side-structural.

\section{The exact input-side minimizer
(\texttt{matmul/sidemin.py})}\label{sec:minimizer}

\textbf{Problem.} Given the 23 signed factor rows of one side
(vectors in $\{-1,0,+1\}^9$; rows equal to a basis vector or to
$\pm$another row are free), find the minimum number of steps of the
form $v = \pm x \pm y$ ($x, y$ earlier values, starting from the 9
basis vectors) such that every distinct multi-term row appears among
the values up to global sign. This is Sun's chain-covering structure
as a search problem.

\textbf{Method.} Iterative deepening on the number of \emph{helpers}
$h$ (chain values that are not target rows); the optimum is
(\#distinct multi-term targets) $+\, h^{*}$. The search uses what we call the
\textbf{closure normal form}: because the pool of computed values
only ever grows, covering a currently-derivable target can never
hurt a later step and always costs exactly one addition --- so the
search covers every derivable target greedily until none remains
(the \emph{closure fixpoint}) and branches \textbf{only on which
helper value to insert at a fixpoint}. Any optimal chain can be
reordered into this form (an exchange argument: coverable-target
steps commute forward past helper insertions), so restricting the
search to it loses nothing. With one helper left, the helper must
directly complete some uncovered target, shrinking the last level to
an ``enabling set''. Helpers may be arbitrary integer vectors,
doublings included --- strictly more general than the pure/one-aux
model of Sun's certificates, so an exhaustion at slack $h$ here is
the stronger impossibility statement.

\textbf{Calibration against Sun's own certificates.} His verifier
proves each of his sides optimal by a reachability argument (no
12-addition chain covers $U$'s 12 targets; no 12-addition chain with
any single auxiliary covers $V$'s 11). Our search independently
reproduces all of it --- $U$: 12 targets, $h^{*} = 1$, 13 additions
(3 search nodes); $V$: 11 targets, $h^{*} = 2$, 13 additions (11
nodes); $h = 0$ and $h = 1$ exhaustions match his two impossibility
certificates --- in 0.3\,s total, every chain replay-verified.
Micro-optima (5 hand-checkable cases) also pass.

\textbf{Scoring a scheme.} Sign models are enumerated by sign-SAT
with blocking clauses and $\mathbb{Z}$-verified; per model the two input sides
are minimized exactly and the output side is optimized by
replay-verified restart-greedy CSE (the v1 pair extractor --- on the
relevant representatives its counts coincide with the v2
optimizer's, and the output side is where all remaining heuristic
slack lives). Side costs were sign-model invariant on every
representative tested.

\section{The exact output-side minimizer (transposition
principle)}\label{sec:trans}

The output side computes the 9 result entries
$C_{pq} = \sum_m W_{pq,m} M_m$ from the 23 products: a linear map $W$,
a $9\times23$ ternary matrix. Greedy pair-extraction CSE minimizes it
only approximately (an upper bound). The exact minimum is the
\emph{shortest linear straight-line program} for $W$ --- NP-hard in
general, and the flank on which \texttt{cxlb}'s SAT encoding
(\S\ref{sec:limits}) stalls.

\textbf{The transposition principle} (Tellegen; standard in the
linear-circuit and signal-processing literature) resolves it. For an
addition-only linear map --- only $\pm$ of earlier values, no change
of basis, negation free --- the minimum additions of a map and its
transpose differ by a fixed constant:
\[
  A(W) = A(W^{\mathsf T}) + \bigl(\mathrm{inputs}(W) -
  \mathrm{outputs}(W)\bigr).
\]
(We verify the constant on hand-checkable cases: for $M$ with
$y_1=x_1{+}x_2$, $y_2=x_2{+}x_3$, $A(M)=2$ and $A(M^{\mathsf T})=1 =
2+(2-3)$; \texttt{tcmin.py}'s selftest checks four such.) For the
output side $W$ is $9\times23$ (23 inputs, 9 outputs), so
$\mathbf{A(W) = A(W^{\mathsf T}) + 14}$, and $W^{\mathsf T}$ is a
23-output map over \textbf{9 inputs} --- \emph{exactly the shape the
input-side minimizer solves exactly}, and small ($A(W^{\mathsf T})
\approx A(W) - 14 \approx 16$). Hence
\[
  \text{exact output side} = \texttt{sidemin}(\text{rows of }
  W^{\mathsf T}\text{ over 9 dims}) + 14,
\]
computed in milliseconds. It is \textbf{constructive}: transposing
the $W^{\mathsf T}$ addition chain by the standard adjoint (reverse
the program; fan-out $\leftrightarrow$ addition) yields an explicit
$A(W^{\mathsf T}){+}14$-addition program for the 9 outputs, which
\texttt{matmul/tcmin.py} emits and which we verify independently
(Appendix~A).

\textbf{Impact.} On Sun's own representative the exact output side is
30 --- equal to his greedy count, so \textbf{Sun's scheme is
output-optimal and 56 is optimal for his representative} (this also
settles the open \texttt{cxlb} bracket of \S\ref{sec:limits} at 30).
But on Perminov's \texttt{cn122} representative (class
\texttt{i19w225c4efh}) the exact output side is \textbf{28}, where
greedy CSE returns 29 --- the missing addition greedy could not see.
Combined with the exact input sides 13+14, that is a
\textbf{55-addition scheme}, verified in Appendix~A. \texttt{tcmin.py}'s
selftest pins both numbers (Sun 30, cn122 28) and the transposition
constant.

\section{Orbit-wide input-side floors and the localization of
55}\label{sec:floors}

The de Groote sandwich $(P A Q^{-1},\, Q B R^{-1},\, R\,\Ct\,P^{-1})$
factors by side: the $A$-form multiset depends only on $(P,Q)$, $B$
on $(Q,R)$, $C$ on $(R,P)$. So three $168\times 168$ tables of
\emph{exact GF(2) side costs} (XOR chain covering --- same algorithm
on 9-bit masks, sub-ms per cell) plus a min-plus scan cover
\textbf{every representative of an orbit}: $168^3 \approx 4.7$M
sandwiches per slot variant, 28.4M per class, in ${\sim}$minutes ---
no hill-climbing, no misses. A GF(2) side cost lower-bounds the
$\mathbb{Z}$ side cost of the same representative (reduce any
$\mathbb{Z}$-chain mod 2), so the scan yields sound orbit-wide side
floors (\texttt{matmul/orbitscan.py}).

\item a 55 with sides 26--27 in Sun's class is excluded up to the

\item a 55 with sides 28--29 requires an output side of 26--27 ---

\end{itemize}

above its own side floor; a 55 there needs sides 27 + $C$ 28. These
are now \emph{concrete, finite} search targets rather than
open-ended hunts.

  such check appears in his note. Perminov's schemes are database
  classes too (IDs above), as is Sun's; one of Perminov's non-record
  schemes (\texttt{naive88}) even lands in one of \emph{our} v1
  classes (\texttt{i107w189c48ae}).

  orbit proposals per class $\times$ 16 sign models $\times$ 128

\section{Limitations and the route to 54}\label{sec:limits}

\textbf{Both sides are now minimized exactly} --- input sides by
chain covering (\S\ref{sec:minimizer}), output side by transposition
(\S\ref{sec:trans}) --- so the total for any given representative is
its true additive complexity in this model, not a heuristic upper
bound. That closes the flank on which \texttt{cxlb}'s SAT lower bounds
(built earlier for the greedy-era open output side) were needed:
transposition supersedes it for exact minimization and confirms
\texttt{cxlb}'s calibration (Sun's output side is exactly 30). What
remains open is \textbf{whether 54 exists}:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Exact re-score for 54.} Every campaign total in
  \S\ref{sec:results}--\S\ref{sec:census} used greedy output sides
  (upper bounds); re-scored with the exact two-sided minimizer they
  can only drop. A 54 needs, e.g., input sides 26 + output 28, or
  27 + 27. The floor-26 classes (best input sides) and the floor-27
  classes are the prime territory; the exact re-score
  (\texttt{matmul/tcscore.py}) over their representatives is the
  direct hunt. So far the exact totals are: sun56 $56=13{+}13{+}30$,
  cn120 $56=13{+}16{+}27$, cn122 \textbf{55}$=13{+}14{+}28$, cn119 56,
  MWS 58, Stapleton 60.
\item \textbf{Database-wide exact re-score.} The orbit machinery
  already charted input-side floors over all 17{,}376 classes
  (\S\ref{sec:census}); pairing those with exact output sides on the
  low-floor classes is the systematic search for a second record.
\item The $r{=}22$ question (challenge 4) remains open and is where
  any count would change complexity theory rather than constants.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Appendix B: the side-minimizer on a worked
example}

Rows (targets) over base variables $a, b, c, d$: $T_1 = a + b$ and
$T_2 = a + b + c + d$. Both have $\ge 2$ terms, are distinct up to
global sign, and neither is a basis vector, so $\mathrm{nt} = 2$
and any chain needs at least 2 additions.

\textbf{Slack $h = 0$} (no helpers --- every addition must create a
target): the pool starts as $\{a, b, c, d\}$. $T_1 = a + b$ is
derivable (both operands in the pool): cover it; the pool grows to
$\{a, b, c, d, a{+}b\}$. Now $T_2$ must be $x \pm y$ with $x, y$
in the pool: subtracting each pool value from $T_2$ gives $c{+}d$,
$a{+}c{+}d$, $b{+}c{+}d$, $a{+}b{+}c$, $a{+}b{+}d$ --- none in the
pool, so the closure is at a fixpoint with $T_2$ uncovered, and
$h = 0$ is \textbf{exhausted: 2 additions are impossible.}

\textbf{Slack $h = 1$}: at that same fixpoint the \emph{enabling
set} --- values $u$ that would directly complete an uncovered target
as $T_2 = x \pm u$ --- is $\{c{+}d,\ a{+}c{+}d,\ b{+}c{+}d,\
a{+}b{+}c,\ a{+}b{+}d, \dots\}$; of these, $u = c + d$ is itself
derivable from the pool ($c$ and $d$ are basis vectors). Insert it
as the helper, and the closure resumes: $T_2 = (a{+}b) + (c{+}d)$.

The optimum is $\mathrm{nt} + h^{*} = 2 + 1 = \textbf{3
additions}$, found in 3 search nodes; the emitted chain is

\begin{Code}
w0 = a + b        (covers T1)
w1 = c + d        (helper)
w2 = w0 + w1      (covers T2)
\end{Code}

\noindent replay-verified by expansion. Global signs are free
throughout: a row $-a - b$ is the same target as $a + b$ (a chain
value may be used negated at no cost).

\section*{Appendix C: $\mathbb{Z}$-scoring a scheme, end to end
(sun56)}

The pipeline of \S\ref{sec:minimizer} on the record
representative, with every number below reproduced by
\texttt{sidemin.py --models 24 perminov\_cache/bits/sun56.bits}:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Mod-2 gate.} The 621-bit vector satisfies all 729
  Brent equations over GF(2).
\item \textbf{Sign-SAT.} The support has 175 nonzero coefficients
  (175 sign variables); the 729 integer Brent equations have 453
  covering terms in total, and the instance has 4{,}269 clauses. Two
  concrete equations: one type-3 equation is covered by $k = 3$
  terms (the products $M_2$, $M_{17}$, $M_{23}$) with right-hand
  side 1, so \textbf{exactly $(3-1)/2 = 1$ of the three terms must
  be negative}; a neighboring rhs-0 equation is covered by $k = 4$
  terms ($M_2$, $M_{17}$, $M_{20}$, $M_{23}$), so exactly 2 of 4 are
  negative. kissat solves the instance in milliseconds; each
  returned model is $\mathbb{Z}$-verified (all 729 equations, exact
  integer arithmetic) before use.
\item \textbf{Exact input sides} (model m0). The 23 $A$-rows
  contain 12 distinct multi-term targets; $h = 0$ is exhausted and
  $h^{*} = 1$ succeeds: $A$-side $= 12 + 1 = \textbf{13}$ additions
  (3 search nodes). The $B$-rows contain 11 distinct targets; $h =
  0$ and $h = 1$ are exhausted, $h^{*} = 2$ succeeds: $B$-side
  $= \textbf{13}$ (11 nodes). These reproduce Sun's own input-side optima
  and impossibility certificates.
\item \textbf{Greedy output side.} Deterministic pair extraction
  gives 31 additions --- its first extraction is $w_0 = M_{13} -
  M_{23}$, a signed pair shared by 3 of the 9 output forms.
  Randomized tie-breaking restarts improve this: best over 300
  restarts $= \textbf{30}$ (the value used throughout; every
  emitted program is replay-verified).
\item \textbf{Total.} $13 + 13 + 30 = \textbf{56}$, on every one
  of the 24 enumerated sign models.
\end{enumerate}
