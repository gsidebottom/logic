\section{The statement being decided}

Fix $r = 22$. The decision problem is the existential sentence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tensor}
  \exists\;\al{1},\dots,\al{r},\;\be{1},\dots,\be{r},\;\ga{1},\dots,\ga{r}
  \in \F^{3\times 3}\quad\text{such that}\quad
  \bigoplus_{m=1}^{r} \al{m}\otimes\be{m}\otimes\ga{m} \;=\; \T ,
\end{equation}
where $\oplus$ is entrywise addition in $\F$ (XOR) and $\T$ is the
\emph{matrix-multiplication tensor}: a fixed $9\times9\times9$ bit array
indexed by an entry position of $A$, one of $B$, and one of $C$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:T}
  \big(\T\big)_{(a,b),(c,d),(p,q)} \;=\; \iv{b=c}\wedge\iv{a=p}\wedge\iv{d=q},
  \qquad a,b,c,d,p,q\in\{1,2,3\}.
\end{equation}
$\T$ has a 1 exactly where the monomial $A_{ab}B_{cd}$ occurs in the
product entry $C_{pq}$ --- the 27 naive terms $A_{pk}B_{kq}$ --- and 0
at the other 702 positions.

\paragraph{Meaning of the unknowns.}
Each triple $(\al{m},\be{m},\ga{m})$ describes one multiplication.
$\al{m}$ selects which entries of $A$ are summed into its left factor,
$\be{m}$ which entries of $B$ are summed into its right factor, and
$\ga{m}$ which output entries receive the product:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scheme}
  M_m \;=\; \Big(\sum_{a,b}\al{m}_{ab}\,A_{ab}\Big)\Big(\sum_{c,d}\be{m}_{cd}\,B_{cd}\Big),
  \qquad
  C_{pq} \;=\; \sum_{m=1}^{r}\ga{m}_{pq}\,M_m .
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:tensor} says that \eqref{eq:scheme} computes
$C = AB$ identically --- for symbolic, non-commuting $A$ and $B$ --- using
$r$ multiplications. ``Rank 22'' is literally the number of rank-one
tensors in the sum. A summand with $\al{m}=0$ contributes nothing, so
the sentence asks for rank $\le 22$. The unknowns are
$3\times 9\times 22 = 594$ bits.

\section{The same sentence as a system of equations (Brent)}

Reading \eqref{eq:tensor} coordinate by coordinate gives one equation
per index tuple $(a,b,c,d,p,q)\in\{1,2,3\}^6$ --- \textbf{729 equations},
each an XOR of $r$ three-way conjunctions equated to a constant:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:brent}
  \boxed{\;
  \bigoplus_{m=1}^{22}\Big(\al{m}_{ab}\wedge\be{m}_{cd}\wedge\ga{m}_{pq}\Big)
  \;=\; \iv{b=c}\wedge\iv{a=p}\wedge\iv{d=q}
  \;}
\end{equation}
These are the \emph{Brent equations} over $\F$. They split by their
right-hand side:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{27 target equations} (RHS $=1$; the tuples with $b=c$,
  $a=p$, $d=q$): each naive term $A_{pk}B_{kq}$ must be generated by an
  \emph{odd} number of the 22 multiplications.
\item \textbf{702 cancellation equations} (RHS $=0$): every other cross
  term $A_{ab}B_{cd}$ must be generated an \emph{even} number of times,
  so that it cancels in every output.
\end{itemize}

Algebraically, \eqref{eq:brent} is a system of 729
\textbf{multilinear cubic polynomials over $\F$} in 594 unknowns, written
in algebraic normal form (XOR of AND-monomials). The structure is rigid:
every monomial takes exactly one variable from each of the three factors
of a \emph{single} product index $m$, and every equation contains exactly
one monomial per product. Logically it is a pure $\exists$ sentence over
594 propositional variables; the only parameters are $r=22$ and the
constant tensor $\T$.

\paragraph{What a verdict would mean.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{SAT}: a rank-22 scheme over $\F$ --- a genuine discovery.
  Heule--Kauers--Seidl lift such $\F$ solutions to $\mathbb Z$ by a
  Gr\"obner-basis step that rarely fails, so it would very likely give
  an integer scheme beating Laderman's 23.
\item \textbf{UNSAT}: no scheme over $\F$, hence none with integer
  coefficients (an integer scheme reduces mod 2 to an $\F$ scheme of
  rank $\le 22$). It would \emph{not} rule out schemes over fields of
  odd characteristic, or rational schemes with even denominators.
\end{itemize}
Today $r=22$ is open in both directions: Laderman's 23 is the best
upper bound, Bl\"aser's 19 the best lower bound.

\section{The symmetry group}

The solution set of \eqref{eq:brent} is invariant under a large group,
which is what licenses the ``without loss of generality'' pins of the
stratum instances.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Product relabeling} $S_{22}$: the sum in
  \eqref{eq:tensor} does not care about the order of its summands.
\item \textbf{Change of basis.} For $U,V,W\in GL_3(\F)$ (168 elements
  each), substituting $A\mapsto UAV$, $B\mapsto V^{-1}BW$ turns a scheme
  for $AB$ into a scheme for $U(AB)W$; undoing that on the output gives
  a new scheme for $AB$. On the coefficient triples the substitution
  acts by \emph{sandwiching},
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:sandwich}
    (\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\;\longmapsto\;
    \big(U\tr\alpha\,V\tr,\;\; V^{-\mathsf T}\beta\,W\tr,\;\; U^{-1}\gamma\,W^{-1}\big),
  \end{equation}
  applied to every summand $m$. The probes use the $W=I$ slice --- the
  \emph{A-side sandwich} $\alpha\mapsto U\tr\alpha V\tr$,
  $\beta\mapsto V^{-\mathsf T}\beta$, $\gamma\mapsto U^{-1}\gamma$ of
  \texttt{strata.py} and \texttt{Rank22Covering.lean} --- which already
  moves $\alpha$ by an arbitrary pair of invertible matrices on its two
  sides.
\item \textbf{Tensor symmetries} $S_3$: $\T$ is invariant under cycling
  the roles of $A$, $B$, $C$ (because $\operatorname{tr}(ABC)$ is cyclic,
  with a transpose on the output slot) and under transposition
  $(A,B,C)\mapsto(B\tr,A\tr,C\tr)$.
\end{enumerate}
The rank of $\al{m}$ is unchanged by \eqref{eq:sandwich} (it multiplies
$\alpha$ by invertible matrices), so the multiset of $\alpha$-ranks is an
invariant of a solution's orbit. That is the handle for the root split.

\section{The root strata (cube-and-conquer, level 1)}

Split on $\rho = \min_m \rank\al{m}\in\{0,1,2,3\}$ and canonicalize the
minimizing summand into slot 1 with \eqref{eq:sandwich}. Each stratum is
\eqref{eq:brent} plus a small conjunction:
\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}l l l@{}}
\toprule
stratum & added constraint & reading \\
\midrule
$S_0$ & $\al{1}=0$ & a dead product --- the rank-21 instance \\
$S_1$ & $\al{1}=E_{11}$ & a rank-1 factor, canonicalized to the corner matrix \\
$S_2$ & $\displaystyle\bigwedge_{m}\rank\al{m}\ge 2\ \wedge\ \al{1}=\operatorname{diag}(1,1,0)$
      & no rank-0/1 factor anywhere; witness of rank 2 in slot 1 \\
$S_3$ & $\displaystyle\bigwedge_{m}\det\al{m}=1\ \wedge\ \al{1}=I_3$
      & every factor invertible; witness in slot 1 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The rank predicates are themselves small Boolean formulas over the nine
bits of a $3\times3$ matrix:
\begin{align}
  \rank\alpha\ge 2 \;&\equiv\; \bigvee_{\substack{i<k\\ j<l}}
     \big(\alpha_{ij}\alpha_{kl}\oplus\alpha_{il}\alpha_{kj}\big)
     \qquad\text{(some $2\times2$ minor is nonzero; 9 minors)},\label{eq:rank2}\\
  \det\alpha = 1 \;&\equiv\; \bigoplus_{\sigma\in S_3}
     \alpha_{1\sigma(1)}\,\alpha_{2\sigma(2)}\,\alpha_{3\sigma(3)}
     \qquad\text{(six permutation terms; signs vanish over $\F$)}.\label{eq:det}
\end{align}
\textbf{Covering.} Every solution of \eqref{eq:brent} is carried by some
element of $S_{22}\times GL_3(\F)^2$ into one of $S_0,\dots,S_3$ (this is
the \texttt{covering} theorem of \texttt{Rank22Covering.lean};
\texttt{brent\_perm} is proved, the sandwich and canonical-form lemmas
are the formalization queue). Hence four certified UNSAT leaves would
prove \eqref{eq:tensor} false. Measured so far: each stratum ran
\texttt{hydra\_satsuma} (satsuma symmetry breaking + kissat, SR-certified)
for 2\,h without a verdict, as did the unstratified $r=22$ instance at
4\,h and the satisfiable $r=23$ control at 6\,h --- level~1 of the tree
is not decidable at hours scale.

\section{Wound instances (the calibration family)}

Take a known rank-23 scheme $(\hat\alpha,\hat\beta,\hat\gamma)$, delete
one of its products, and freeze $n_{\mathrm{fix}}$ of the remaining 22
summands at their known values:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:wound}
  \exists\,(\al{m},\be{m},\ga{m})_{m\notin P}\;:\;
  \bigoplus_{m\in P}\hat\alpha^{(m)}\otimes\hat\beta^{(m)}\otimes\hat\gamma^{(m)}
  \;\oplus\;
  \bigoplus_{m\notin P}\al{m}\otimes\be{m}\otimes\ga{m} \;=\; \T,
  \qquad |P| = n_{\mathrm{fix}} .
\end{equation}
Only $22-n_{\mathrm{fix}}$ summands are free, so the instance asks
whether a local repair of the wound exists. (``Wound'' as in injury:
the metaphor is to take a healthy rank-23 scheme, wound it by deleting
one of its 23 products, freeze most of the survivors, and ask the
solver whether the few free products can heal the damage --- complete
the tensor with 22. The term is this project's coinage, not standard
terminology; the closest published relative is the deletion/repair or
completion notion in the flip-graph literature.) This family is how the
solver was priced: 414 attacks, 273 certified UNSAT and 141 timeouts,
with difficulty rising roughly $15\times$ for every 4 additional free
products and the 90-second frontier sitting at
$n_{\mathrm{fix}}\approx 14$--$16$.

\section{Down to CNF, in one paragraph}

The only step below this level is Tseitin. Each pair-AND
$\al{m}_{ab}\wedge\be{m}_{cd}$ gets a definitional variable (81 per
product, shared across the nine $\gamma$ entries), each triple is that
variable $\wedge\,\ga{m}_{pq}$, and each 22-term XOR in \eqref{eq:brent}
is chained through 3-input XOR gadgets. That is how 594 variables become
the 31{,}941-variable, 126{,}117-clause \texttt{brent\_3x3x22.cnf}, and
why conflict-driven search struggles: the parity structure is invisible
to clause learning, and Gaussian elimination cannot reach through the
AND guards. Heule--Kauers--Seidl's diagnosis (average backtrack level
above 100, runtime roughly exponential in it) holds here too: local
search with streamliners finds rank-23 witnesses, but nothing so far
produces an UNSAT certificate at rank 22 --- which is exactly why the
certified tree splits the problem by symmetry instead of asking a
solver to swallow it whole.
